Bitter Melon, Gymnema and Banaba: Three Traditional Botanicals

Where bitter melon, gymnema and banaba come from, how they've been used traditionally, what modern research suggests and safety points.

Published 2026-11-25 · GLPro information site

Three of the botanicals in GLPro come from very different parts of the world but share a long history of traditional use around blood sugar. Here's a closer look.

Bitter melon (Momordica charantia)

Bitter melon is a ridged green gourd eaten as a vegetable across Asia, Africa, South America and the Caribbean. NIH's LiverTox resource describes its traditional use for blood sugar and notes that extracts are usually well tolerated, with possible stomach upset and low blood sugar as side effects. NCCIH says research is still too limited to show it is effective.

Gymnema sylvestre

Gymnema is a climbing plant used in Ayurveda for centuries. It's best known for temporarily dulling the taste of sweetness when the leaves are chewed, which is why it's linked with sugar cravings. LiverTox's gymnema entry reviews its traditional uses and notes rare, isolated reports of liver injury from products containing it.

Banaba (Lagerstroemia speciosa)

Banaba is a flowering tree from Southeast Asia whose leaves are traditionally brewed as a tea. They naturally contain corosolic acid, a compound researched for its role in glucose uptake by cells.

Why combine them?

Each botanical has been studied for a slightly different angle: absorption, cravings or glucose uptake. GLPro brings them together with cinnamon, chromium and alpha lipoic acid. See the sugar cravings focus area and the ingredients list.

Because these botanicals may lower blood sugar, people taking diabetes medicine should speak to a doctor before using them.
