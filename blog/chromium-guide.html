Chromium Picolinate: What It Does and What the Evidence Shows

Chromium helps insulin do its job. Learn what NIH says about chromium supplements, food sources, safety and why results in studies are mixed.

Published 2026-11-25 · GLPro information site

Chromium appears on the ingredient list of many blood sugar supplements, including GLPro, usually as chromium picolinate. Here's what this trace mineral does and what the evidence really shows.

What chromium does

According to the NIH Office of Dietary Supplements chromium fact sheet, chromium appears to enhance the action of insulin, the hormone that helps move glucose into your cells.

What the studies show

The fact sheet also notes that trials of chromium supplements for blood sugar control have produced inconsistent results. NCCIH's diabetes and supplements page reports that some reviews suggest modest improvements in markers such as HbA1c, while others find little effect.

Food sources

Chromium is found in small amounts in broccoli, whole grains, meat, potatoes, green beans and some spices. Most people get some chromium from a varied diet.

Safety

Chromium from food is considered safe. Rare problems have been reported with very large supplemental doses, and chromium may interact with some medicines, including insulin and other diabetes drugs. Talk to your healthcare provider if you take these.
