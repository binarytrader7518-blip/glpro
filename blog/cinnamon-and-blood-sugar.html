Cinnamon and Blood Sugar: What the Research Actually Says

Cinnamon is a popular blood sugar ingredient. Here's what government research summaries say, the cassia vs Ceylon question and safety tips.

Published 2026-11-25 · GLPro information site

Cinnamon is probably the most familiar ingredient in blood sugar supplements, GLPro included. But what does the research say, and is all cinnamon the same?

What the research says

The NIH's National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health (NCCIH) summarises that it's unclear whether cinnamon supplements help with diabetes or weight, and that better-quality studies are needed. Its broader review of diabetes and dietary supplements notes that some studies have found reductions in fasting blood glucose, while others haven't.

In short, cinnamon is a reasonable supporting ingredient, best seen as one part of a broader approach.

Cassia vs. Ceylon cinnamon

Most supermarket cinnamon is cassia. It contains more coumarin, a natural compound that can affect the liver in large amounts or in sensitive people. Ceylon cinnamon (“true cinnamon”) contains much less. One GLPro sales page names Ceylon cinnamon bark specifically.

Easy ways to enjoy cinnamon

  • Stir it into plain yoghurt or porridge instead of sugar.
  • Add it to coffee for sweetness without the sugar.
  • Use it in savoury dishes such as chilli, tagines and curries.
