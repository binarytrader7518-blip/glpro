How it works

How GLPro Works

How the ingredients in GLPro are designed to support your body's normal glucose metabolism, and how to use it day to day.

The Short Version

After a meal, carbohydrates are broken down into glucose and enter your bloodstream. Insulin, released by the pancreas, helps move that glucose into muscle, liver and fat cells where it's used or stored. GLP-1 is a gut hormone released after eating that's involved in insulin release and the feeling of fullness; that's why the GLPro label describes it as a “Natural GLP-1 Booster”. Our GLP-1 explainer covers what that phrase can and can't mean.

GLPro brings together ingredients that have been studied for their roles in this everyday process: how cells respond to insulin, how quickly carbohydrates are absorbed, and how strongly you crave sweets. It's designed to support these normal functions, not to replace medication or override them.

Ingredient roles

What Each Ingredient Contributes

Cinnamon bark

Studied for its relationship with glucose uptake and insulin signalling. Government reviews call the evidence mixed, so think of it as a supporting player.

Chromium picolinate

Chromium is a trace mineral that helps insulin do its job. Studies of supplements show inconsistent results.

Bitter melon

Contains plant compounds researched for insulin-like activity, mainly in lab and animal studies.

Gymnema sylvestre

Traditionally linked with reducing the perception of sweetness, which is why it's associated with cravings.

Banaba leaf

A natural source of corosolic acid, a compound studied for its role in moving glucose into cells.

Alpha lipoic acid

A cofactor in mitochondrial energy production with antioxidant properties.

Research on single ingredients, often in labs or animals, doesn't prove the finished GLPro formula will produce a particular result. See the references for the evidence behind each one.
